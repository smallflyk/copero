## What is Pokémon Champions Regulation M-B?

Pokémon Champions Regulation M-B was the game’s second organized-play ruleset. The collected rules reference dates its first use from June 17, 2026 until September 2, 2026. It limits eligibility to a defined Pokémon list, permits held items without duplicates, enables selected Mega Evolutions, and allows only one Mega Evolution per player in a battle.

A regulation defines what teams can legally enter, while the metagame describes what players actually choose. This page keeps those ideas separate: dates, timers, item duplication, and Mega limits are rules; Charizard, Garchomp, Floette, weather, and Perish Song are dated strategy observations. The in-game legality screen should be the final check if a maintenance notice changes anything.

## Regulation M-B rules and timers

The total time limit is 20 minutes. Each player has a seven-minute player timer, each turn has a 45-second limit, and team preview lasts 90 seconds. These values are stated directly in the collected ruleset and should not be replaced by estimates from a different Pokémon title.

Held items are allowed, but duplicate held items are not. That means two team members cannot carry the same item in a legal M-B roster. Select Pokémon can Mega Evolve, but a player can perform Mega Evolution only once per battle even when the team contains multiple possible Mega choices.

## Eligible Pokémon and Mega choices

Eligibility is restricted to the regulation’s published list rather than every Pokémon that can exist in a player’s broader collection. The source includes familiar species across generations and a separate legal Mega Evolution section. Availability in Pokémon Champions, transfer support through Pokémon HOME, and M-B legality are three related but different questions.

The official metagame overview discusses Mega Charizard Y, Mega Aerodactyl, Mega Floette, Mega Scovillain, Mega Froslass, Mega Tyranitar, Mega Swampert, Mega Delphox, Mega Blastoise, Mega Mawile, Mega Gengar, and several off-meta choices. This is not the entire legal Mega list. It is the subset highlighted by the official strategy article because of tournament use or distinctive roles.

## Mega Charizard Y and the “Big Six”

The official overview says Mega Charizard Y dominated early Pokémon Champions tournaments. Garchomp remained a key partner because Earthquake does not hit the Flying-type Charizard, while Heat Wave and Ground coverage pressure different targets. One early Charizard variation with Venusaur and Incineroar won the first Pokémon Champions Regional Championship in Indianapolis.

Two later Charizard structures became especially important. One used Mega Aerodactyl as a second possible Mega and added Farigiraf for flexibility between Tailwind and Trick Room while Armor Tail blocked priority. Another used Whimsicott and Mega Floette; players called this high-usage structure the “Big Six,” and the official article says it occupied five Top 8 places at Japan Nationals, including first.

## The leading non-Mega Pokémon

The official analysis highlights Sneasler, Garchomp, Basculegion, Kingambit, and Incineroar. Sneasler combines Dire Claw and Close Combat coverage with Fake Out support. Garchomp is described as the format’s leading Ground option and appeared throughout tournament top cuts, with Life Orb, Choice Scarf, Roseli Berry, and Sitrus Berry among its documented items.

Basculegion is presented as a late-game sweeper built around Last Respects and Adaptability. Kingambit checks it with Sucker Punch and commonly chooses among Kowtow Cleave, Protect, Low Kick, Swords Dance, and Iron Head. Incineroar lost U-turn and Knock Off in Champions but retained valuable Fire/Dark typing, Intimidate, Fake Out, and Parting Shot support.

## Mega Floette and setup teams

Mega Floette’s Fairy Aura boosts Fairy-type moves on the field. Its Light of Ruin is documented at 140 base power and 90 accuracy, with recoil equal to half the damage dealt, making Tailwind support valuable for an aggressive version. The official article also notes Calm Mind variants supported by Incineroar and Sinistcha, sometimes using Draining Kiss for recovery.

Other setup teams take different routes. Mega Delphox uses Nasty Plot with Incineroar and Sinistcha, while Swords Dance Kingambit provides an alternate win condition. Mega Blastoise uses Shell Smash with Water Spout and Dark Pulse, often receiving healing support from Sinistcha’s Hospitality because Water Spout depends on remaining health.

## Weather teams in Regulation M-B

Sun, snow, sand, and rain all appear in the official M-B overview. Mega Charizard Y and Venusaur represent a familiar sun path, while Mega Pyroar can use sun from Torkoal and stack Fire damage through Fire Mane. Mega Froslass sets snow through Snow Warning and can establish Aurora Veil or fire an accurate Blizzard on the turn it Mega Evolves.

Mega Tyranitar offers sand control and a strong answer to Mega Charizard Y, although Garchomp remains a problem it must address. Rain lacks a Mega Pokémon with Drizzle in this format, so the article describes manual Rain Dance or switches into Pelipper or Politoed. Archaludon benefits through immediate Electro Shot, and Mega Swampert uses Swift Swim and rain-boosted Wave Crash.

## Mega Gengar and Perish Song

Mega Gengar’s Shadow Tag can prevent non-Ghost opponents from switching. Perish Song starts a three-turn countdown, so trapping creates a direct endgame plan when the opponent cannot clear the effect by switching. Incineroar, Sinistcha, and Politoed are identified as typical partners, with Politoed adding a second Perish Song option.

The official article also describes balance teams that use Shadow Tag for positioning rather than only a Perish finish. Alolan Ninetales can pair Encore with Gengar’s Disable, and a tournament-winning Turin variation used Swords Dance Kingambit, two sources of Fake Out pressure, and Floette. These are recorded structures, not guaranteed wins.

## Off-meta Mega Evolutions

Mega Staraptor uses Contrary to turn Close Combat’s normal defensive drops into boosts and can receive further stat changes from Whimsicott. Mega Scrafty can reset Intimidate by Mega Evolving, enabling it to apply the Attack reduction twice while also offering Fake Out. Mega Kangaskhan appears as an alternative on Charizard/Whimsicott or hard Trick Room teams.

Mega Glimmora was explored for Adaptability and coverage from Power Gem, Sludge Bomb, and Earth Power. Mega Raichu Y had more documented impact than Mega Raichu X in the official snapshot, using No Guard with Zap Cannon and Focus Blast. Those observations describe results available at the article date and may shift with later tournaments.

## How to build legally for M-B

Start with the official eligibility list, then assign held items without duplicates. If the roster contains multiple Mega candidates, decide which matchups make each one the actual Mega while remembering that only one can transform. Use the 90-second preview to identify weather, speed control, trapping, priority blocking, and likely leads.

Plan turns within the 45-second decision limit and watch the separate seven-minute player timer. A legal team is not automatically a coherent one: it still needs damage, speed control, defensive switching, and a way to finish. Use tournament structures as evidence of workable patterns, then adjust for the matchups you actually encounter instead of copying one list as timeless truth.
